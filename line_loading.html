▸ How to read this
P99 is the level exceeded in 1% of hours, not a typical value. Read it with the median and the hours>90% column: Bannaby–Sydney has a P99 of 95.4% but sits at a 41.5% median and is above 90% in only 147 hours (1.7%), while Molong–Orange North is above 90% for 888 hours (12.7% of the year). A high P99 on a low median is a peaking profile or a rating artefact; a high P90 and a high hour count is a line that runs hard.
Loading = |flow| ÷ the seasonal rating the operator applied in that snapshot, so it says how hard the line was actually run — not how much headroom remained after a contingency. Ratings are real seasonal values: summer means sit ~7% below winter.
Suspect ratings (purple flag) are circuits whose rating swings more than 3× over the year or carries a 7999/9999 "no limit modelled" sentinel. Their loading percentages are not usable — Blue Gum–Mortlake reads 308% against a rating that ranges 359–3,053 MVA.
One row per physical circuit. A circuit moves between PSS/E bus-pair labels as bus sections are switched; labels are merged only where they are never live in the same hour, so parallel plant is never welded together. Coverage is the share of the record in which the circuit was in service and modelled — a low value means new or part-year plant, not missing data. Source: reports/hourly_circuit_loading_by_season.csv.
